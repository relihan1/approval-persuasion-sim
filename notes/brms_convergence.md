# Primary model: convergence diagnosis and history

This note keeps the detail behind the primary-model section of the README: the earlier formula's full-size fits, the diagnosis that changed script 05, and the first full-size check of the current formula. Every result describes the simulation's assumed model, not people.

## The earlier formula's full-size fits

The four full-size fits in this section used an earlier, smaller formula: no palter terms (the request intercepts absorbed paltering's average cost, and the contrasts dropped the palter weights), no participant slope for tactics, and one request-intercept SD for safe and unsafe requests alike. A full-size fit of that formula (900 participants, 43,200 reviews, four chains of 2,000 iterations) took 208 minutes on four cores when it had the machine to itself (the cue state); the other three, run alongside each other and the simulations, took several hours each. Every fit uses the same simulated study (seed 5) and the same Stan seed, and rerunning two of them reproduced their posterior summaries exactly. Script 05b compared the verdicts; the tables are archived in `notes/brms_earlier_formula/` (`brms_rule_comparison.csv` there):

| True state | Primary model, 95% rule | Primary model, rule matched to the fast estimator | Fast estimator, participant-clustered | Fast estimator, two-way with jackknife | Largest R-hat | Smallest bulk ESS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cue | Cue | Cue | Cue | Cue | 1.010 | 212 |
| Vigilance | Vigilance | Vigilance | Vigilance | Both | 1.038 | 139 |
| Neither | Neither | Neither | Neither | Neither | 1.013 | 276 |
| Both | Both | Both | Both | Both | 1.024 | 172 |

R-hat and ESS are for the location coefficients. The primary model reached the right verdict in all four states under either rule. The fast estimator with jackknifed two-way errors called the vigilance study "both" because it falsely detected a cue effect. At this sample size, that happened in 6.4% of vigilance-state studies in script 02 (`output/tables/verdicts_two_way_jackknife.csv`).

These fits need more work before their intervals can be trusted to the second decimal: Stan warned of low bulk and tail effective sample sizes in every fit, and the largest R-hat was above 1.01 in three of the four. And one fit per state says nothing about the primary model's error rates. At 3.5 hours or more per fit, the 100 fits that 25 per state would need are out of reach on one workstation.

## Diagnosis and changes to script 05

A diagnosis of the earlier formula at a third of full size (50 participants per cell, the same 48 requests, cue state) found two problems and changed script 05 accordingly:

- **Starting values.** brms computes each review's likelihood as log(Φ(*a*) − Φ(*b*)), which underflows to log(0) when a linear predictor lands far from the thresholds. Of 200 starting points drawn the default way (every unconstrained parameter uniform on −2 to 2), 2 had a finite log density at full size. Stan tries 100 per chain and drops a chain that finds none: at a third of full size, two of the four chains were dropped and brms returned the other two with only a warning. Reproducing the four full-size fits' starting values (same data and seeds) shows that all their chains started, but chain 3 started at a log density near −250,000, against −120,000 to −135,000 for the others; the fits weren't saved, so whether that was the chain that ran long can't be checked. Script 05 now draws starting values from −0.5 to 0.5 (`init_r = 0.5`), where 141 of 200 were finite, and saves each fit to `output/fits/` (not under version control).
- **What mixes slowly.** With all four chains started, there were no divergent transitions, no iterations at the tree-depth limit, and E-BFMI was 0.62 to 0.69. The slowest parameters were the thresholds (bulk ESS 711 of 4,000 draws) and the coefficients that vary only between participants or only between requests (training, screen, unsafe; 829 or more, and an R-hat of 1.012 for training × evidence screen). These trade off against the means of the random intercepts and have no weight in any contrast. The six contrasts had R-hat of 1.001 or less and bulk ESS of 2,673 or more. The request-slope SDs, whose true value is 0, mixed well (bulk ESS above 2,200). Refits of the same data confirmed that the usual remedies have nothing to fix: half-normal(0, 0.25) priors on those two SDs, or dropping the request-effect correlations, left the cost per draw and the mixing of the slowest parameters within run-to-run variation, and `adapt_delta = 0.99` (tree-depth limit 12) took 2.3 times as many gradient evaluations for worse mixing (contrast bulk ESS 1,546, against 2,673).

Script 05 now checks R-hat (below 1.01) for every parameter class except the individual participant and request effects, and ESS for the contrasts, the SD of unsafe-request evidence, and the random-effect SDs (at least 400 from four chains; Vehtari et al., 2021), and records divergences and E-BFMI (`R/brms_check.R`, `brms_convergence_*.csv`, and a `converged` column). A full-size fit now runs 2,000 sampling iterations per chain after 1,000 of warmup, because the earlier full-size fits reached bulk ESS of only 139 to 276 for the location coefficients from 4 × 1,000 draws. Setting `BRMS_THREADS` splits each chain across several threads, which shortens a single fit when cores are free but doesn't raise throughput for a batch: at full size, four threads per chain made each gradient evaluation 3.2 times faster (one chain of 200 iterations, on a machine partly busy with other jobs), so a fit on 16 cores would take under a third of the time it takes on 4, at about 25% more core-hours.

The current formula, fitted to the same data at a third of full size with the same settings, also had no divergent transitions (E-BFMI 0.67), and its contrasts moved by at most 0.006 (T1: −0.687 against −0.693); the palter terms recovered their generating values (−0.53 for paltering and +0.56 for its removal by the evidence screen, against −0.6 and +0.6). It cost 36% more gradient evaluations, because its step sizes were smaller (0.043 to 0.057, against 0.080 to 0.094). The two chains with the smallest step sizes (0.043 and 0.045, against 0.054 and 0.057) needed twice as many evaluations per iteration as the other two (127 against 63) and took twice as long to sample, with no divergences and the same E-BFMI: a chain can run twice as long as the others for no worse reason than a slightly smaller adapted step size. Its slowest parameter was the SD of participants' susceptibility to tactics, which is 0 in the simulation (bulk ESS 237 from 4 × 1,000 draws).

## First full-size check of the current formula

One full-size fit of the current formula with the current settings (vigilance state, the same simulated study, four threads per chain, on a machine shared with other jobs) passed the convergence rule and reached the right verdict, as did the fast estimator on the same data. It had no divergent transitions and E-BFMI of 0.70 to 0.77. The largest R-hat was 1.0096, for the request-intercept SD; among the classes with an ESS floor, the smallest bulk ESS was 861, for the SD of susceptibility to tactics, and the contrasts' was 3,070 or more. The SD of unsafe-request evidence had a posterior median of 1.25, its generating value. It passed only just: with the first 1,000 of each chain's 2,000 sampling iterations, the same fit fails the rule (R-hat 1.023 for training × paraphrase screen and 1.011 for the request-intercept SD), so the extra iterations were needed, and some fits in a batch should be expected to fail and need longer chains. Every chain adapted to a step size of 0.028 to 0.038 and took 127 gradient evaluations per sampling iteration, and warmup averaged 204 to 233 per iteration: about 470,000 evaluations per chain, about 45% more than a projection from a third of full size had suggested. The fit took 3 hours 55 minutes (per chain, 113 to 124 minutes of warmup and 107 to 111 of sampling), at 28 to 29 seconds per 1,000 evaluations on four threads. At the single-thread cost of an evaluation of the earlier formula at full size (77 milliseconds), a chain would take at least 10 hours on one core, so the 100 fits of a 25-per-state batch would need at least 4,000 core-hours.
